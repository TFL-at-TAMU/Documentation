The Cricut Explore 4 is a desktop cutting machine: instead of a printer's ink head, it drags a small blade across sheet material to cut out any shape you can draw. Use it for vinyl decals and stickers, iron-on transfers for shirts and bags, paper and cardstock models, labels, stencils, and card-stock packaging. It can also draw on your material with a pen instead of cutting it. It works on sheets up to **12 in** wide (or 12 in × 24 in on the long mat) and material up to **2.0 mm** thick — anything thicker, or anything rigid like plywood, metal, or acrylic, belongs on another machine. If you're not sure this is the right machine for your project, ask a staff member.

> [!WARNING]
> **Keep hands, hair, and loose clothing out of the machine while it's running.** The cutting head moves fast and without warning, and the blade in it is genuinely sharp. Don't reach in to straighten material mid-job — pause first. Only handle blades when the machine is stopped, and hold the housing, not the blade.

:::caution[EMERGENCY STOP]
Press the **Pause button (9)** on the right side of the machine — it stops the head immediately, mid-cut. If that doesn't work, unplug the power cord at the **lower right of the machine's back**, then tell a staff member what happened.
:::

> [!NOTE]
> **No PPE is needed for this machine** — no eye protection, no gloves, no hearing protection. It's one of the lowest-risk machines in the lab. Standard Fab Lab attire, with long hair tied back, is enough.

## Before you start

- **No training or checkout is required.** Read this page, and ask a staff member if anything here doesn't match what you see at the machine.
- **Check what material the lab has on hand.** The Fab Lab keeps some vinyl, cardstock, and mats in stock, but it varies — ask a staff member what's available before you plan around it. You're welcome to bring your own material.
- Your material must be **no thicker than 2.0 mm** and appear on [Cricut's material list for Explore machines](https://help.cricut.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009504773-Which-materials-can-I-cut-with-my-Cricut-smart-cutting-machine#explore-machines) (open the **Cricut Explore Machines** section, then the **Cricut Explore 3 & Explore 4** tab). If your material isn't on that list, ask staff before cutting it — an unsupported material usually just means a ruined cut and a chewed-up mat, but it can break the blade.
- Most material needs a **cutting mat**. Only Cricut **Smart Materials** — the ones whose packaging says "Smart" — load into the machine without one.
- Leave at least **12 inches of clear space behind the machine.** The mat feeds all the way out the back during a cut and will shove anything in its way.
- You'll design in **Cricut Design Space**, which is free. Either use the lab computer, or bring your own laptop — see [Setting up Design Space](#setting-up-design-space) below.

## Machine overview

![Diagram of the Cricut Explore 4's controls and indicator lights](../assets/images/the_fab_lab_cricut_o_b7d5067722.png)

The controls you'll actually use are the four buttons on the right-hand side — **Power (6)**, **Load/Unload (7)**, **Go (8)**, and **Pause (9)** — plus the two tool clamps behind the cover:

- **Clamp A** holds accessories that draw or score, like the fine-point pen. It's empty for a plain cut.
- **Clamp B** holds the blade housing. The **Premium Fine-Point Blade** lives here and handles almost everything: vinyl, iron-on, paper, cardstock, and light fabric.

The button lights tell you what the machine is waiting for. The **Power** button glows white when it's on, **blue** once it's connected over Bluetooth, purple while it updates, and red on an error. The **Load** and **Go** buttons flash white when it's your turn to press them.

## Setting up Design Space

You only do this once per computer.

**On a lab computer:** open **Cricut Design Space** — it's already installed. Sign in with your own free Cricut account, or ask staff which account the lab uses.

**On your own laptop:** go to [design.cricut.com](https://design.cricut.com/), download Design Space, and install it. When it asks which machine you have, choose **Explore 4**. Create a free Cricut account when prompted.

Either way, the machine connects over **Bluetooth**, so turn Bluetooth on and keep your computer **within 15 feet** of the Cricut.

## Operating

1. Press the **Power button (6)** on the right side of the machine. It glows white when the machine is on, and turns blue once your computer connects.

2. Open the machine's cover — press the **release button on the left side** of the machine (the one with the raised ridges) and lift. The cover has to be open to reach the tool clamps and to load a mat.

3. Open your project in Design Space, or start a new one. When it's ready to cut, click **Make** in the top right.

4. Choose how your material loads. Pick **On Mat** for almost everything — vinyl, iron-on, paper, cardstock. Pick **Without Mat** only for Cricut Smart Materials.

   ![Design Space asking "How will you load your materials?", with On Mat and Without Mat options](../assets/images/the_fab_lab_cricut_o_ead4ec9c77.png)

   Then confirm the **mat size** matches the physical mat you're holding — a 12 in × 12 in mat needs the 12 in × 12 in setting, or your design will be cut in the wrong place.

5. Put your material on the mat. Peel the clear plastic film off the mat and **keep it somewhere safe** — it goes back on at the end to protect the adhesive. Press the material down firmly, lined up with the same corner Design Space shows it in.

6. Wait for Design Space to connect to the machine — the top of the screen will name it, like **Explore 4 : 0364 Bluetooth**. If it doesn't find the machine, see [Common problems](#common-problems).

7. Set your **base material**. Design Space suggests a few, but the exact material matters — it sets the blade pressure — so click **Browse All Materials** and search for what you're actually cutting.

   ![The Set Base Material step in Design Space, with a material picker and a Browse All Materials link](../assets/images/the_fab_lab_cricut_o_b8e7f6d2f0.png)

8. Load the tools Design Space asks for. It shows you exactly what goes in each clamp — usually nothing in **Clamp A** and the fine-point blade in **Clamp B**.

   ![Design Space's three Make steps: set base material, load tools and material, press Go](../assets/images/the_fab_lab_cricut_o_61591f4341.png)

   To load a tool, push the clamp's lever open, drop the housing in so its collar rests on the clamp, and close the lever. If the blade is already in Clamp B, just open and close the clamp without changing anything — that's enough to satisfy the prompt.

   ![Clamp A and Clamp B closed, with the blade housing seated in Clamp B](../assets/images/the_fab_lab_cricut_o_0c722af3ea.jpeg) ![Clamp B open with the blade housing removed](../assets/images/the_fab_lab_cricut_o_43aa47aac4.jpeg)

9. Load the mat. Hold it flat against the **material guides (11)** and push it up against the rollers, then press the flashing **Load button (7)**. Keep holding the mat until the rollers grip it and pull it in — if you let go early it will feed in crooked and the cut will be off.

10. Press the flashing **Go button (8)** to start the cut. Stay with the machine while it runs.

> [!WARNING]
> Stop the machine with the **Pause button (9)** and get a staff member if you hear grinding or knocking, if the blade is dragging or tearing the material instead of cutting it, or if you smell burning. Don't try to fix a job in progress with your hands in the machine.

11. When the cut finishes, press the **Load/Unload button (7)** again to release the mat.

12. Peel your material off the mat rather than lifting the mat off the material — bending the material away from the mat keeps paper and vinyl from curling and tearing. Put the clear plastic film back on the mat.

## Finishing up

- Put the plastic film back on the mat and return the mat, blade, and any lab material to where you found them.
- Clear scraps out of the machine and off the table — small offcuts fall into the cutting area and jam the next person's job.
- Turn the machine off with the **Power button (6)** and close the cover.
- Tell a staff member about anything that went wrong, even if you worked around it.
- Take your project and your material with you — the lab has no storage.

## Common problems

**Design Space can't find the machine.** Check that the Cricut is powered on and its power button is lit, that Bluetooth is on, and that you're within 15 feet of it. If it still won't appear, pair it from your computer's own Bluetooth settings first, then go back to Design Space. Failing that, unplug the Cricut, plug it back in, and reopen your project.

**The cut is in the wrong place, or runs off the edge of the material.** Almost always the mat loaded crooked or the mat size in Design Space doesn't match the physical mat. Unload, re-seat the mat squarely against the material guides, hold it until the rollers take it, and check the mat size in the Make screen.

**The blade didn't cut all the way through.** The base material setting is probably wrong — a heavier material needs more pressure. Set the exact material in **Browse All Materials** rather than a near-enough one. If it's still short, the blade may be dull or the mat may have lost its tack; ask staff before swapping a blade.

**Material tears or lifts while cutting.** The material isn't stuck down well enough, or the mat has lost its grip. Press it down firmly across the whole sheet before loading. If the mat no longer holds paper at all, tell staff — mats are consumable and get replaced.
